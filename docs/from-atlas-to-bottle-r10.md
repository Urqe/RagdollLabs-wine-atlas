# R10 — From Atlas to Bottle

Implemented 2026-10-22 as a 12-profile pilot. This release connects a modeled style to a possible bottle or wine-list entry. It does not scan labels, recommend a merchant, certify a particular bottle or change the atlas's 150 profiles and scores.

## What a reader sees

A collapsed **Recognize it on the label** section appears only for Chablis Chardonnay; Cabernet-led and Merlot-led Bordeaux; Valpolicella Classico and Amarone; Prosecco DOC; Fino, dry Oloroso and Pedro Ximénez Sherry; and Vidal, Riesling and Cabernet Franc Niagara Icewine. Each gives an example label/list wording, what that wording supports, what it does not establish, and what to verify with a producer or seller. Links lead to represented alternatives; selected terms open the existing glossary dialog with focus returned on close. Guided flights direct bottle selection to the linked profile where available. The original profile URLs and search classifications are unchanged.

Examples are reading aids, not fabricated labels for a particular producer. Bordeaux AOC alone cannot select the Cabernet- or Merlot-led reference. Chablis is Chardonnay, but the Chablis AOC profile does not guarantee steel-only ageing. Valpolicella, Ripasso, Amarone and Recioto cannot be swapped by name; the latter two are dried-grape styles with different sweetness contexts. Prosecco Extra Dry is sweeter than Brut and the represented dry-to-off-dry tank-method style is not every Prosecco bottle. A short 'Sherry' listing needs a style and sweetness check. VQA Niagara Icewine needs its region and grape checked to choose one of the represented profiles; Late Harvest is a different category.

Four glossary entries cover phylloxera, grafted vines, own-rooted/ungrafted vines, and pre-/post-phylloxera wording. The last entry gives two specifically attributed examples: [Quinta do Noval Nacional](https://www.quintadonoval.com/en/vin/12/nacional-vintage-port) and [Bollinger Vieilles Vignes Françaises](https://www.champagne-bollinger.com/en/the-collection/vieilles-vignes-francaises). These are named bottlings tied to small documented parcels, not generic atlas Port or Champagne profiles. Ungrafted does not date a vine; a modern ungrafted planting, surviving old vines and wine bottled before an outbreak are different claims. No single global cutoff, taste fingerprint, quality bonus or 'pure wine' claim follows.

## Claim sources and boundaries

The label cases use direct, dated, reviewed entries in `data/sources.json`, including [Chablis appellations](https://www.chablis-wines.com/discover/a-unique-territory-and-terroir/four-appellations-and-one-varietal/four-appellations-and-one-varietal,1807,7644.html), [Bordeaux AOC diversity](https://www.bordeaux.com/en/designations/bordeaux-bordeaux-superieur-and-cremant/bordeaux/), the [Valpolicella consortium](https://www.consorziovalpolicella.it/en/types-of-wines/), [Amarone](https://www.consorziovalpolicella.it/en/types-of-wines/amarone-della-valpolicella-docg/), [Prosecco DOC types](https://www.prosecco.wine/en/prosecco/discover/types/), the [Sherry Consejo classifications](https://www.sherry.wine/sherry-wine), and [Ontario VQA categories and label rules](https://vqaontario.ca/vqa-wine-program/wine-categories-and-labelling/). Scope is each named designation or category, not an inference about all wines in a country. The [Australian Wine Research Institute phylloxera guidance](https://www.awri.com.au/industry_support/viticulture/pests-and-diseases/phylloxera/) supports root damage and grafting; producer pages support only their own parcel/bottling examples. Source IDs and claim scope are in `data/label-guidance.json` and `data/sources.json`. All R10 source reviews are dated 2026-10-22. Pre-existing regional background references remain distinct from these claims.

## Implementation and verification

`label-guidance.js` loads optional curated JSON and renders text as DOM nodes. Missing or malformed guidance disappears without interrupting profile browsing. The Python validator blocks unknown profiles, alternatives, glossary terms, missing cue/limit fields, unreviewed sources and unsourced vineyard examples. DOM regressions cover all 12 cases, alternative links, glossary focus restoration, absent and malformed data, safe text rendering, and flight signposting. `npm run validate`, GitHub validation and Pages deployment passed. In deployed desktop Chrome, the Chablis disclosure rendered legibly without horizontal overflow; Bordeaux's alternative link opened the Merlot reference and focused its heading; the glossary showed the two scoped vineyard examples; the unpiloted Napa profile omitted the section. The lead-in was then tightened to distinguish label wording from confirmed producer details.

Mobile/touch, macOS/iOS Safari, real screen readers, enlarged text, and actual novice bottle-identification trials remain open. This release does not claim they were tested by a DOM harness.
